Why Lacuna

Every business deserves what the enterprise takes for granted

Twenty years inside this industry taught us that the gap between what a global enterprise receives and what a fifty-person business receives has very little to do with technology. Both run the same operating systems, the same cloud platforms, the same security tools. The difference is what the provider is willing to commit to.

Large organisations have the leverage to insist on published response times, tested recovery, real reporting and a penalty when it slips. Everybody else is handed a brochure and told to be grateful for it. That is not a technology problem, it is an expectations problem — and it has been allowed to stand for a very long time.

Read your current agreement. There is a response time in it, and almost certainly nothing that happens if it is missed. That gap — between what was promised and what anyone is actually accountable for — is where poor service lives, and closing it is the reason we exist.

We built Lacuna to do exactly that. To bring businesses of any size the standard of service that, until now, only the largest ones have had the power to demand.

Raising what you expect from a provider matters more to us than winning your contract.

The problem

Most IT problems are not technical

They are accountability problems. Nobody is measuring how long anything takes, nothing happens when it slips, and eventually your staff stop reporting faults at all — so the real cost never appears on anyone's report.

Your people stopped telling you

When raising a ticket feels pointless, people work around problems instead. Ask around and you will find someone who has quietly given up — and whatever they have stopped reporting is invisible to everyone above them.

Nobody can say how long things take

No ticketing means no record, and no record means no argument. The problems that get fixed are the ones raised by whoever is most persistent, rather than the ones costing you the most.

The invoice is never the real cost

Forty people losing half a day is roughly $6,000 in salaried time. Six of those a year dwarfs almost any support contract — it is simply harder to see, because it never arrives as a bill.

You have become the escalation path

When something serious breaks, the person chasing it is you. That is the clearest sign the arrangement is not working, and it is the part no provider ever puts on a report.

Our approach

Five stages, in this order

Switching provider is the genuinely risky part, not the running. This is how we take that risk instead of handing it to you.

01Recognise — evaluate, understand, engage

We audit what you actually run, not what the last provider documented — every device, every licence, every system, every dependency, including the things nobody remembers buying. It takes two weeks and roughly two hours of your team's time in total, and you get the written assessment regardless of whether you go any further with us.

  • Full inventory of devices, licences, systems and connectivity
  • Recovery position tested rather than assumed
  • Risks ranked by business impact, not by technical severity
02Consult — advise, collaborate, aim

We show you what we found and agree the order things get fixed in. You set the priorities — the commonest bad experience with a new provider is being told what matters rather than being asked. Everything is costed before anything starts, so there is no point at which the number moves without you having agreed to it first.

03Adapt — align, design, prepare

The gaps carrying real risk get closed first, on a plan you can see. We run in parallel with your existing arrangement for the first thirty days, and nothing is switched off until we have proven it working on our side.

  • Parallel running so there is no cliff edge
  • Documentation produced as we go, and it is yours
  • Your current provider stays until the handover is proven
04Outcomes — transform, implement, deliver

Changes land and we report what moved. Not activity, not ticket volumes — the specific risks that are now closed and the ones still open with a date against them. This is also the point at which the service credits and response times start applying, so the commitments become measurable from day one rather than after a settling-in period.

05Manage — proactive, maintain, improve

Proactive monitoring, published response times, monthly reporting and a quarterly review with someone who can actually make decisions. The estate gets a forward plan rather than drifting until something fails. And if any of this stops being true, three consecutive months of missed service levels lets you give notice and leave — which is how you hold us to it rather than having to argue about it.

Questions

The things people ask first

How much does managed IT actually cost?

Around $250 per user per month for a full managed service. The exact figure depends on what you run — how many servers, how much of the estate is cloud, what compliance obligations you carry — and we give you a fixed number after the assessment. What it includes matters as much as the number, and ours covers unlimited support with no per-incident billing, so the monthly figure is what you actually pay rather than a starting point.

What size business do you work with?

Ten users and above. Below that the economics genuinely do not work in your favour and you would be better served by a local contractor — we will tell you so rather than take the work. The exception is regulated businesses: a six-person dental practice or financial advisor carries obligations that a sixty-person marketing agency does not, and those we are happy to talk to at any size.

What are your response times?

P1, meaning the business is stopped with no workaround, carries a fifteen minute target. P2 is one hour, P3 is four hours, P4 is one business day. Response means a named engineer working on the problem, not an automated acknowledgement — and all four are written into the agreement, with the first three carrying service credits if missed.

How do service credits actually work?

If we miss a published response time, a credit is applied to your next invoice automatically. There is no claim form, no process, and nothing for you to prove or chase. The amounts are not large, and that is not really the point — the point is that being slow costs us money, which is a materially different arrangement from one where it costs us nothing.

What happens if we want to leave?

At the end of the term, you simply leave. Before then, there is one route out on every agreement including the five year: if we miss your service levels for three consecutive months, you can give three months' notice and end it, with no penalty and no negotiation about what the clause meant. And if you leave by that route, we transition you to your new provider free of charge — having failed you, billing you to go would be indefensible. Your documentation, inventory and configurations are yours throughout in any case. We do not hold estates hostage; a provider who has to rely on that has already lost the argument.

Do you replace our internal IT person?

Usually not. Where there is internal IT we normally take the infrastructure, monitoring, out-of-hours and escalation, which frees your person to work on the things that actually need someone who knows your business. We will agree exactly where we stop and they start, in writing, before anything begins — ambiguity there is what makes co-sourcing arrangements fail.

How long does switching take, and how disruptive is it?

Around ninety days end to end, of which the first two weeks are the assessment and require about two hours of your team's time. We run in parallel with your existing arrangement for the first thirty days and nothing is switched off until we have proven it working on our side, so your current provider stays in place until the handover is complete.

Where are you based?

Lacuna is a UK registered company serving clients in the United States. Support is delivered remotely by our service desk, with onsite attendance arranged where your agreement includes it. Our privacy policy covers both UK and US data protection requirements, and we are happy to walk your compliance team through the arrangement before you commit to anything.
